Record.Wait.Decrypt.

Every blockchain is a permanent public copy. Anyone can store every transaction today and wait for a quantum computer that runs Shor's algorithm. On that day, the elliptic-curve keys that hide receivers and amounts in today's privacy coins stop hiding anything that was ever recorded.

howapaymentworks

On chain, a payment is a gap.

A Kyber transaction proves that value moved without saying who sent it, who received it, or how much. This is what a wallet does to make one, and what the chain keeps. The panel shows the latest private transaction on the live testnet.

● 01 · share an address
The receiver hands over a quantum address

A kybq1 address carries the spend key, the view key and an ML-KEM-768 public key. At about 1,700 characters it's long, so wallets copy it whole.

● 02 · derive a one-time address
The sender's wallet makes a fresh secret

It encapsulates a shared secret to the receiver's ML-KEM key. From that secret it derives a one-time address and a blinding key no one else can compute.

● 03 · seal the amount
The amount becomes a commitment

The value is hidden in a Pedersen commitment, encrypted to the receiver, and backed by a 48-bit range proof so nobody can mint coins from a negative amount.

● 04 · sign with sixteen
The wallet signs inside a ring

It picks 15 decoys from the chain, builds an MLSAG signature over all 16 and publishes a key image. All of this happens in your browser.

● 05 · mine and scan
One key opens one output

Nodes check the ring, the range proofs, the balance and the key image, and Argon2id miners seal it in a block. The receiver's wallet tries its keys on each new output. One opens.

thingspeoplekeepasking
Questions.Answers.
Is the testnet actually private?

Yes. Every transaction on the live testnet uses a real ring of 16, real one-time addresses, real Pedersen commitments with range proofs and, for kybq1 addresses, real ML-KEM-768. Open the explorer: there are no senders, receivers or amounts to read.

Why keep ring signatures if they use elliptic curves?

They are fast and well studied today. A quantum computer that could break them does not exist yet, but recorded receivers and amounts are the part that leaks backwards in time, so Kyber seals those with ML-KEM first. Faraday replaces the ring with hash-based proofs.

Where are my keys?

In your browser. The wallet creates a 32-byte seed on your device and derives the spend key, view key and ML-KEM key from it. The node never sees them. Back up the seed.

What is the difference between tKYBER and $KYBER?

tKYBER is the testnet coin from the faucet and has no value. $KYBER is the Solana token that funds the work and carries the project to mainnet.

Can I run a node?

Yes. The node, explorer and wallet are one open-source Node.js program. Clone the repository, run npm install and npm start, and you have the full chain on your machine.

When does mainnet launch?

After Faraday is specified, tested and reviewed in the open. Follow @KyberChainL1 for dates.
